On the validation folds all three tuned models reach a weighted F1 of about 0.9999 (Logistic Regression 0.9999 ± 0.0001, Decision Tree 0.9999 ± 0.0001, Random Forest 0.9999 ± 0.0001), so this metric cannot rank them. Macro F1, in which FTP-BruteForce counts as much as Benign, separates them: Logistic Regression 0.9793 ± 0.0133, Decision Tree 0.9903 ± 0.0192 and Random Forest 0.9955 ± 0.0089 (Figure 4.2). The out-of-fold confusion matrices (Figure 4.3) show where the errors are: Logistic Regression made 9 errors (5 Benign as FTP-BruteForce; 1 Benign as SSH-Bruteforce; 3 SSH-Bruteforce as Benign), the Decision Tree 9 (1 Benign as FTP-BruteForce; 4 Benign as SSH-Bruteforce; 1 FTP-BruteForce as SSH-Bruteforce; 3 SSH-Bruteforce as Benign) and the Random Forest 4 (1 Benign as FTP-BruteForce; 3 SSH-Bruteforce as Benign), out of 60,000 flows. The same 3 SSH-Bruteforce flows are predicted as Benign by all three models, so these errors come from the flows themselves rather than from any one algorithm: each has one or two packets, no payload and an initial forward window of 241 bytes instead of the 26,883 seen in almost all other SSH flows, so it shares none of the traits the models rely on. Errors involving FTP-BruteForce are few (5, 2 and 1), but with only about 7 FTP flows per validation fold a single one moves macro F1 noticeably, which is why macro scores vary between folds (Decision Tree FTP recall per fold: 0.86, 1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 1.00). Training-fold scores are at or near 1.0 for the trees (Random Forest macro F1 1.0000 ± 0.0000), so the forest memorises its training data, but its validation scores stay almost as high, which indicates little harmful over-fitting. Tuning against weighted F1 changed macro F1 by +0.0074 for Logistic Regression (the search chose the symlog transform), -0.0051 for the Decision Tree and +0.0000 for the Random Forest: because weighted F1 is saturated, it selects almost arbitrarily among near-identical settings, a problem I return to in section 5.
