On the validation folds every tuned model reaches a weighted F1 between 0.9996 and 1.0000, so this metric cannot rank them. Macro F1, in which FTP-BruteForce counts as much as Benign, separates them more: from 0.9793 (Logistic Regression) to 0.99998 (k-Nearest Neighbours; Figure 4.3, Table 4.2). The out-of-fold confusion matrices (Figure 4.4) show between 1 error (k-Nearest Neighbours) and 22 (Gaussian Naive Bayes) out of 60,000 flows. The same 2 SSH-Bruteforce flows are predicted as Benign by at least seven of the eight models, so these errors come from the flows rather than from any algorithm: each has one or two packets, no payload and an initial forward window of 241 bytes instead of 26,883, so it shares none of the traits the models rely on. With about 7 FTP flows per validation fold a single error moves macro F1 noticeably, which is why macro scores vary between folds. Training-fold scores are at or near 1.0 for most models; the largest gap between training and validation macro F1 is 0.0096 (Decision Tree), so no model over-fits badly. Compared with the default settings, tuning raised macro F1 by more than 0.001 for k-Nearest Neighbours, Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machine, Gradient Boosting and lowered it by more than 0.001 for Decision Tree, Multi-layer Perceptron: because weighted F1 is saturated, it selects almost arbitrarily among near-identical settings, a problem I return to in section 5.
