I compare three algorithms that make different assumptions about the decision boundary. Logistic Regression (multinomial, L2-regularised) is the linear baseline: it fits one linear score per class and converts the scores to probabilities with the softmax function. If it performs almost as well as the trees, the classes are close to linearly separable. Because its coefficients become unstable when inputs are strongly correlated, as the EDA found, I tune its regularisation strength. A Decision Tree (CART) splits the data recursively on single-feature thresholds that most reduce impurity. Such rules match the patterns found in the EDA (zero payload for FTP-BruteForce, a fixed 640-byte packet for SSH-Bruteforce), are unaffected by scale and heavy tails, and can be read directly, but an unpruned tree over-fits, so I tune its depth and leaf size. A Random Forest (Breiman, 2001) averages many trees, each grown on a bootstrap sample with a random subset of features considered at every split; averaging de-correlated trees reduces the variance of a single tree, which matters with only 37 FTP-BruteForce training flows. All three are implemented in scikit-learn (Pedregosa et al., 2011) inside one imbalanced-learn pipeline (Lemaître, Nogueira and Aridas, 2017).
