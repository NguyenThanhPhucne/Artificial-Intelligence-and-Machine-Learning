Every model is the same imbalanced-learn pipeline: Protocol, a nominal attribute, is one-hot encoded; the numeric features pass through an optional symlog transform, a filter that drops constant columns and a scaler (standardisation, or for k-NN optionally min-max normalisation); SMOTE then oversamples FTP-BruteForce; finally the classifier is fitted. Because imbalanced-learn applies SMOTE only during fitting, synthetic flows never reach a validation fold. I first scored each algorithm with scikit-learn's default hyper-parameters and no SMOTE, then tuned each with RandomizedSearchCV: 20 settings × 5 stratified folds = 100 fits per model, with the SMOTE target and neighbour count searched alongside the model parameters, and the setting with the best mean weighted F1 refitted on all 60,000 training flows (Table 4.1). An exhaustive GridSearchCV over the same Decision Tree ranges (2,160 fits in 494 s, against 100 fits in 20 s) reached a best weighted F1 of 0.99987 against 0.99985, so random sampling lost almost nothing at a fraction of the cost. On an M1 Max refitting the selected model took from 0.27 s (k-Nearest Neighbours) to 7.03 s (Multi-layer Perceptron), and inference from 0.37 ms (Decision Tree) to 32.73 ms (k-Nearest Neighbours) per 1,000 flows (Figure 4.2). Weighted F1 barely discriminated between settings: for 8 of the 8 algorithms at least 5 of the 20 settings scored within one standard deviation of the best.
