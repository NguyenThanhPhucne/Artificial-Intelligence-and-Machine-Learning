The exploration answers the three questions and fixes the modelling choices that follow. (Q1) Once duplicates are removed, the problem is severely imbalanced (0.06% FTP-BruteForce), so I evaluate with macro-averaged F1 and per-class recall alongside weighted F1, and I oversample the minority class with SMOTE inside the cross-validation pipeline, capped well below the majority size because the class has only 37 training flows. (Q2) The hypothesis is supported: payload and header-length features separate both attacks from benign traffic almost perfectly, so near-perfect scores for Benign and SSH-Bruteforce are expected and are not by themselves evidence of a good detector. The real tests are the rare FTP class and whether the models depend on shortcuts. I therefore exclude Dst Port and Timestamp from the main model and run ablations that add Dst Port back and remove the initial window sizes. (Q3) Features are heavy-tailed and partly redundant (9 of the 190 pairs among the 20 highest-variance features have |r| ≥ 0.95), which favours tree-based models that are insensitive to scale and correlated inputs, and calls for standardisation and regularisation for Logistic Regression; I keep outliers because they are genuine traffic. Because 471 feature pairs are rank-correlated at |rho| ≥ 0.7, I also test correlation-based feature selection inside cross-validation (Step 5).
